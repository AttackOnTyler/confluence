# Squad Brawler RPG Design Brief: Lessons from X-Men Legends and Marvel: Ultimate Alliance (2004–2019) for a New Original IP

The genre is worth reviving. Across five games the formula held up: four heroes on screen, switch between them at any time, drop-in co-op, light RPG progression, and a big cast crossing over. Critics and fans kept praising the roster, the co-op and the combination attacks. They kept criticizing the same things too: the camera, repetitive corridor levels, and progression that was either too thin (MUA2) or too opaque and grindy (MUA3). An original IP can't lean on Marvel nostalgia, so it has to win on two things. First, a crossover premise that turns "many distinct heroes in one squad" into the core fantasy. Second, a progression system that keeps the whole roster worth playing.

## TL;DR
- **What made the formula work:** a 4-hero squad you can swap between at any time, 1–4 player drop-in co-op, a small set of powers per hero, and team combination attacks (XML's Super Combos, MUA1's Xtreme powers, MUA2's Fusions, MUA3's Synergies). The fun came from team building and crossover spectacle, not mechanical depth. Scores reflect that: X-Men Legends (82 Metacritic, Xbox), XML2 (84, Xbox), MUA1 (82, PS2), MUA2 (74 PS3 / 73 360 on Metacritic) and MUA3 (73 Metacritic / 73 OpenCritic, 49% of critics recommend).
- **What held it back:** the camera in every entry; linear, repetitive levels; weak AI companions; and progression that swung between MUA1's per-hero gear and costume bonuses, MUA2's stripped-down team-wide "Boosts", and MUA3's grid plus ISO-8 crystals, which one fan summed up as "simultaneously brain-dead simple but also stupidly complex". In MUA3, XP only goes to heroes you use, so most of the roster falls behind.
- **The opportunity:** nobody has made a 3D, squad-based, party-switching co-op action RPG since MUA3 in 2019. Recent hits prove the market is there: *Absolum* (roguelite beat 'em up, 93% positive on Steam), *Marvel Cosmic Invasion* (85% OpenCritic recommend), and *Split Fiction* (4M sold by May 6, 2025, two months after its March 6 launch, per Hazelight; premium, no microtransactions). Meanwhile live-service superhero co-op (Marvel's Avengers, Suicide Squad) failed. The recommendation is a **premium, campaign-first, drop-in co-op squad ARPG with an original "multiversal crossover" premise**, a roguelite-style replayable endgame, and no gear grind or monetized power.

## Key Findings

1. **Lineage is continuous and short.** Raven Software built the formula in X-Men Legends (2004) and XML2 (2005), then widened it to the whole Marvel universe in MUA1 (2006). Vicarious Visions took over for MUA2 (2009). Team Ninja rebooted it for Nintendo in MUA3 (2019), after Activision's Marvel license expired in 2014.
2. **Combination attacks are the series' signature, and each entry reinvented them.** XML had Super Combos and Xtreme Powers (unlocked at level 15). MUA1 gated Xtreme attacks behind a momentum meter. MUA2 added pair Fusions, with a unique Fusion for every hero pair. MUA3 used Synergy attacks, with a stronger version when all four heroes sync.
3. **Progression is where the series was least consistent, and the part fans argue about most.** MUA1 gave each hero one gear slot, four costumes with upgradeable bonuses, and team reputation. MUA2 removed gear in favor of up to three team-wide Boosts and automatic stat gains, and GameSpot called the loss of loot "disappointing". MUA3 layered power upgrades, a roster-wide Alliance Enhancement grid, and ISO-8 crystals on top of per-hero XP. That combination pushed players to neglect most of the roster.
4. **"Extremis" is not an MUA2 progression system.** In MUA2, Extremis is an Iron Man costume. The actual systems are Boosts, Heroic Deeds, Power points and four auto-leveled attributes. The brief should not treat Extremis as a mechanic to reference.
5. **Hub worlds did real narrative work in the Raven era.** They were the X-Mansion, XML2's hub that changed each act, and MUA1's Stark Tower and other bases. They held dialogue, trivia, bios, vendors and challenge missions. MUA3 largely dropped this layer.
6. **The market has split.** Small-to-mid-budget co-op brawlers and roguelites are thriving. Big-budget superhero games built on a live-service or grind model have flopped. The squad-switching 3D ARPG sits in an empty space between the two.

## Details

### 1. Core Game Loop

| Game | Mission structure | Hub | Pacing notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **X-Men Legends (2004)** | Linear missions through groups of enemies to an end boss | X-Mansion. You play as newcomer Alison Crestmere (Magma), explore, talk to X-Men, use vendors (Forge for equipment, the Morlock Healer for consumables and training discs), Danger Room challenges, trivia for XP | RPGFan: "Stages are broken up by visits to Xavier's School… This is also where story develops, as most conversation happens in the mansion." |
| **XML2: Rise of Apocalypse (2005)** | Linear acts: Greenland prison, Genosha, Savage Land, Egypt | An enemy-free hub whose setting "changes to coincide with the current act" | Adds Danger Room missions, trivia for XP, New Game+ |
| **MUA1 (2006)** | 17 comic-inspired locales. Optional missions change the ending ("multiple possible endings") | Stark Tower and other bases with side tasks, NPC dialogue, trivia, simulator discs that unlock bonus comic missions | Critics praised it for balancing "the action and RPG elements, while letting players choose just how much they want to micromanage" |
| **MUA2 (2009)** | Act I shared, then a Pro- or Anti-Registration branch, then a shared finale. Two endings | Between-mission bases, with Fallout-style dialogue attitudes | Gaming Nexus: "very little difference between the two paths" beyond roster restrictions and enemy uniforms. Single-player run described as short |
| **MUA3 (2019)** | Linear chapters chasing Infinity Stones, plus the post-campaign Infinity Rifts challenge mode | Minimal hub layer | Kotaku took about ten hours to finish the story on Mighty difficulty. Rifts carry the endgame |

**Takeaway:** a run looks like this: story beat in the hub, then a linear mission of roughly 20–40 minutes with 1–2 mini-bosses and one boss, then back to the hub for upgrades and dialogue. Exploration is light: breakables, collectibles, and environmental puzzles gated by character abilities. The HyperComboGamer XML retrospective gives examples: Colossus "can rip a radiator off a wall," and Iceman builds "ice bridges to reach isolated platforms".

### 2. Combat Systems

- **Shared control scheme across the series:** light attack, heavy attack, jump, block/dodge, grab/throw, and four powers on shoulder button plus face button. XML: "one button for weak attack, one for strong attack, and one to jump. Holding the right trigger and pressing one of the face buttons takes care of the mutant powers… the directional pad switches between the four active X-Men." MUA3: "Each character's controls are fundamentally identical; each has a jump, light attack, heavy attack, dodge, block, and four special abilities."
- **Combos:** light and heavy strings that stun, trip or pop enemies up. MUA1 added grappling, blocking, dodging, chargeable powers, and fighting in the air and underwater.
- **Resources:** energy pays for powers. Powers matter more than melee: StrategyWiki notes for MUA2 that "powers are far more effective than straight-up melee."
- **Ultimate and team attacks by game:**
  - XML/XML2: combos where several players hit one enemy with powers together. "Super Combos" pair specials with an "Xtreme Power", available at level 15.
  - MUA1: Xtreme attacks need a full momentum meter. Momentum orbs refill one hero fully and give the other three 25% each.
  - MUA2: **Fusions.** "Each playable character in the game has a unique fusion with every other playable character" (for example, Captain America reflecting Storm's lightning with his shield). There are three types: **Clearing** (large-radius blasts), **Guided** (a moving area-of-effect you steer) and **Targeted** (single target, best against bosses). They cost Fusion Stars earned from kills. Sources disagree on the cap (two or three stars). The "Powers of Two" achievement requires every pairing across 24 heroes.
  - MUA3: **Synergy** attacks sync specials between heroes, with "an especially powerful attack unleashed if all four party members sync up simultaneously," plus an Extreme gauge for ultimates.
- **Damage types:** MUA2's Boosts refer to Energy, Fire, Electric, Radiation, Crushing, Wounding and Impact damage. XML2 had elite enemies that project auras "grant[ing] other enemies in the vicinity resistance to a specific damage type" (TV Tropes), which is a useful threat-priority mechanic.
- **Stagger/break:** MUA3 power upgrades raise "stagger damage" (WayTooManyGames). Players describe boss fights as "mostly using staggering moves followed by powerful attack moves".
- **Difficulty:** XML's curve was criticized as "a jagged cliff" (HyperComboGamer). MUA2 has Legendary-only Boosts and New Game+. MUA3 gates a hidden fourth power upgrade behind Superior difficulty and Void Spheres. Tom's Guide listed "Big difficulty spikes" and "Frustrating boss fights" as MUA3 cons.
- **AI companions:** mixed. A GameFAQs critic blurb on MUA1 said "The teammate AI is very impressive, often getting to baddies before you even see them, but once again, Raven has designed a game that is better through cooperative play." RPGFan on XML: "AI-controlled characters will fall off of cliffs and kill themselves." GameSpot on MUA2 (Wii) cited "dumb AI" and clumping "awkward clumps of heroes."

### 3. RPG Progression and Itemization

| System | XML1/2 | MUA1 | MUA2 (PS3/360) | MUA3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leveling | XP; points into powers | XP; power points (respec-able); stats **auto-upgrade**; option to auto-spend powers; bench heroes kept about one level behind | Auto-leveled **Strike / Body / Focus / Teamwork** (cap 50 base, level cap 60); one point per level into Powers | Per-hero XP **only when used**; XP-boost items |
| Gear | Equippable items; Forge vendor | **One gear slot per hero**; boss/chest drops; general and hero-specific items | **No gear.** Up to **3 team-wide Boosts** out of **216** | **ISO-8 crystals**: colored and tiered passives with trade-offs (e.g. more damage dealt but also taken); fused and upgraded to +5, then the next tier |
| Cosmetic/stat | — | **4 costumes per hero**, each with bonuses you can level up with currency | 1 alternate costume (appearance only) | Mostly recolors |
| Team layer | — | Custom team name, emblem, **reputation** → Team Vitality (+25% HP max), Synergy (+25% energy), Force (+11% damage), Focus (+24% XP), Tactics (momentum), Bench (roster slots). Swapping heroes costs reputation. Comic team bonuses (Avengers, Fantastic Four, X-Men, etc.) | **Heroic Deeds** (three per hero: two unlock Boosts, one a costume); dialogue attitude unlocks Boosts | **Alliance Enhancement** hex grid (roster-wide stat buys); team bonuses (e.g. four "Web Warriors" = +8% Resilience) |

Critical reaction:
- **MUA2:** GameSpot: "you no longer find and equip gear as you did in the original… It's disappointing to see the role-playing elements of the original stripped down in such a way; after all, finding more loot is an addictive part of similar games." Co-Optimus found team badges "quite inferior to equipping individual characters with specific gear."
- **MUA3, GameSpot:** found "a surprising amount of depth," but "heroes only level up when you use them… you end up neglecting most of the roster because they just aren't powerful enough," and fixing that takes XP boosts that are "a long, grindy process."
- **MUA3, Kotaku:** called it "menu management heaven" but noted only four of 33 unlocked heroes had passed level 40 by the credits.
- **MUA3, users:** Metacritic users called ISO-8 "very confuse[d]" and the upgrade system "needlessly complicated"; they also complained "You never learn new abilities."

**Design implication:** use the MUA1 model. Per-hero identity items plus team-level bonuses, with automatic stats and chosen powers, cause the fewest complaints. Pair it with **shared/benched XP** so that swapping heroes stays viable.

### 4. Roster and Character Design

- **Scale:** XML started with the X-Men and XML2 added the Brotherhood. MUA1 had at least 23 base characters, plus platform exclusives and eight DLC heroes on Xbox 360. MUA2 had 24+ with pro/anti-registration locks. MUA3 had 36 base heroes, 13 paid DLC heroes plus a hidden 14th, and 2 free X-Men.
- **How shared systems still felt distinct:** identical controls, but each hero's four powers differ in shape (projectile, cone, dash, AoE, buff), range (melee versus ranged) and element. Iconic traversal also differs (flyers, swingers, heavy lifters). Environmental interactions are gated by archetype, for example Colossus throwing objects and Iceman bridging gaps. MUA2's pairwise Fusions (276 pairings) created combinatorial novelty, but one reviewer noted "all of the moves are essentially identical" within a fusion type, so players tired of repeats.
- **Composition strategy:** implicit archetypes (brawler, blaster, powerhouse, agile, support/buffer) plus explicit **team-name bonuses** that reward recreating famous line-ups. These bonuses were a favorite system in every entry. Kotaku noted a Venom/Spider-Man/Miles/Spider-Gwen "Web Warriors" line-up earns +8% Resilience.
- **Balance complaints:** mostly about level gaps (MUA3) and weak characters, not tank/DPS/healer roles. There's no hard trinity; "support" is mostly buffs and heals attached to otherwise offensive heroes.

### 5. Co-op Multiplayer

- **Local 1–4 players on one screen** with a shared camera, from XML onward. XML2 added "players can join or leave at any time" and online play for four, a series first, on Xbox Live until April 15, 2010.
- MUA1 supported local four-player play on every version except PSP, with online play on most. MUA2 PS3/360 had PSN/Xbox Live online.
- MUA3 allowed four players on one docked Switch, **four Switch systems locally**, or online with friends and random lobbies.
- **Camera is the recurring co-op tax:**
  - MUA1: "a camera that occasionally obscures the action."
  - MUA3, Den of Geek: "pulling back so far that you can't tell who's who on the battlefield… my characters were frequently killed by projectiles shot from off-screen enemies."
  - MUA3, WayTooManyGames: "the camera almost always fails to keep up… especially in more chaotic boss fights."
  - MUA3, Bleacher Report: particle effects make it "hard to keep track… amplified in handheld mode," plus "framerate hitches."
- **Pacing and balance:** co-op was universally described as the best way to play (Game Chronicles: the co-op "is what really make this game rise above its predecessors"). However, shared-screen chaos cuts readability, and the RPG layer assumes one player managing four heroes. The brief should decide whether each co-op player owns one hero or a sub-squad.

### 6. Narrative and Presentation

- **XML:** an original story told through a newcomer (Magma). Patrick Stewart as Xavier. Cel-shaded visuals praised. Loading-screen art, comic covers and bios collected in the hub.
- **XML2:** a "greatest threat" premise forcing X-Men and Brotherhood to ally against Apocalypse. Blur Studio cinematics. GameSpot's Greg Mueller: "the novelty of teaming up the good guys and bad guys to go after the really bad guys is enough to make this game enjoyable in its own right."
- **MUA1:** an original Doctor Doom/Masters of Evil plot touring Atlantis, Asgard and other locales. Epilogues change depending on optional missions. Trivia and dossiers build the shared-universe feel. Some felt the plot was thin or talky: a Metacritic critic asked for "less of the Chatty-Cathy moments."
- **MUA2:** adapts Secret War and Civil War. Players side with Iron Man (Pro-Registration) or Captain America (Anti-Registration). Includes a Stan Lee cameo and dialogue attitudes (Aggressive/Diplomatic/Defensive) that unlock Boosts. Praised for "branching narrative, improved writing and replay value," but a GameFAQs critic said "the Fusion attacks and half-hearted story hurt the experience."
- **MUA3:** an original Infinity Stones story drawing on more than 20 comic arcs. Den of Geek called the writing "heavy-handed", and IGN called it "a fun and irreverent comic book story." It also offers an over-the-shoulder "Heroic Camera" option.
- **Pattern:** the shared-universe feel came from **villain alliances + location tourism + unlikely-ally team-ups + collectible lore (bios, trivia, comic covers, concept art)**, not from deep dialogue trees.

**Retrospective sentiment.** MUA1 is widely treated as the peak. ScreenRant called it "Marvel's Greatest Game" and noted that Activision's Marvel titles were delisted after the license ended, so MUA1/2 are hard to buy. ResetEra threads looking for "games that scratch that X-Men Legends / MUA1 itch" come up empty and point to Tales/Ys-style party action JRPGs instead. ComicBook.com (2025): "I've been waiting 6 years for a follow-up."

### 8. Genre Lineage and Comparison

- **Ancestors:** Gauntlet (four-player arcade dungeon crawl), 90s arcade brawlers (X-Men arcade), Diablo (loot ARPG), and the Snowblind-engine console ARPGs: *Baldur's Gate: Dark Alliance* (2001, over 1M sold), *Champions of Norrath* and *Justice League Heroes*. Those were one-hero-per-player, loot-driven, and mostly two-player couch co-op.
- **What makes XML/MUA distinct:** (a) **one player can command a whole four-hero squad** with hot-swapping, so single-player feels like party play and co-op feels like splitting a team; (b) **a large, pre-authored hero roster** instead of class creation; (c) **combination attacks between heroes**; (d) **team-composition bonuses**; (e) a crossover narrative. Diablo-likes reward builds and loot, and Gauntlet rewards arcade survival. MUA rewards *assembling a team*.
- **Since 2019:**
  - *Marvel's Midnight Suns* (2022): turn-based and card-driven, "a critical success but a commercial flop" per Take-Two's Strauss Zelnick via Bloomberg.
  - *Marvel's Guardians of the Galaxy*: single-player, where you issue squad commands.
  - *Marvel's Avengers* (2020): live-service, supported just over two years.
  - *Suicide Squad: Kill the Justice League* (2024): live-service; after a 13,459 launch peak, VGC citing SteamDB reported concurrent players "never passed 1,000" in a week (daily average 754), and Windows Central later reported an all-time low of 118.
  - 2D brawler revival: *TMNT: Shredder's Revenge*, *Marvel Cosmic Invasion* (Dec 2025; 15 heroes, tag-team pairs; Metacritic 81 PC / 80 PS5 / 84 XSX; 85% OpenCritic).
  - *Absolum* (2025): roguelite beat 'em up, original IP.
  - Nobody has shipped a **3D, squad-switching, drop-in co-op ARPG with a large roster**.

### 9. Modern Audience Considerations

- **Premium co-op is commercially proven.**
  - *Split Fiction* ($49.99, no microtransactions, free "Friend Pass"): 1M sales in 48 hours, 2M in a week, and 4M by May 6, 2025 (Hazelight: "4 MILLION SOLD!!!!"), which EA's results called "hugely successful." EA said that was nearly double its expectations. Hazelight later reported 7M in about a year and more than 50M across its three games.
  - *Helldivers 2*: an all-time peak of 458,709 concurrent Steam players on February 24, 2024 (SteamDB), on a low-price, cosmetic-focused model.
- **Live-service superhero co-op is proven to fail.** Marvel's Avengers failed; commentators blamed thin content and cosmetic monetization. Suicide Squad followed, and Gotham Knights was criticized for "a sluggish gear grind, level-gating, and repetitive side content."
- **Roguelite structure extends brawler replay.**
  - *Absolum*: 93% positive of 4,711 Steam reviews, $24.99, about 8 hours for a first full run and about 30 hours to unlock everything. Co-op online and local; a joining player's world resets to the least-progressed player, but "all progress carries over."
  - PC Gamer faulted *Cosmic Invasion* for being "a bit too old school," suggesting "a randomised survival mode… alt versions of levels… weird new modifiers."
- **What modern players expect:** progression that respects their time, and no roster neglect (fixes for MUA3's problem). Cosmetics as the only purchases. Crossplay and online drop-in. Readable combat VFX. A campaign of about 10–15 hours plus a replayable endgame.

### 10. Original IP Opportunity: Recommended Angle

**Recommendation: "multiversal crossover squad," built as an urban-fantasy/sci-fi pastiche, not a straight superhero parody.**

- **Why not a pure superhero pastiche:** it invites unflattering comparison with Marvel and DC and has none of their nostalgia. Superhero parody also caps the tone.
- **What MUA actually delivered:** "mash up characters from different corners of a shared universe." An original IP can own this by making the crossover *diegetic*. Example premise: champions pulled from different worlds or genres (a mecha pilot, a fantasy knight, a noir detective with psychic powers, a kaiju-bonded kid) into one hub-city at the junction of realities.
- **What this premise buys:**
  - Visual distinctness for free: each hero's home genre defines their silhouette, VFX palette and damage type.
  - A diegetic reason for team-origin bonuses, e.g. "Realm" sets as the equivalent of Avengers or X-Men bonuses.
  - A hub that grows as allies arrive, like XML2's hub changing each act.
  - Villain alliances across realms, like the Masters of Evil.
- **Hooks that fill the gap:**
  1. **Pair and quad combination attacks as the core verb.** Fusion-style unique pair moves, but authored per *archetype pair* with hero-specific flourishes, to avoid MUA2's sameness and cut the 276-animation cost.
  2. **Roster-wide progression.** Benched heroes earn XP; the upgrade tree is team-wide; each hero has a small number of signature "relic" slots (MUA1-style identity gear).
  3. **Roguelite "Rift" endgame** after a hand-authored campaign, with modifiers and remixed levels.
  4. **Camera-first co-op design:** arena-style combat spaces, optional dynamic split-screen, readability-first VFX.
  5. **Premium price ($30–50), cosmetic-only DLC, paid roster expansions** (MUA3-style), with no power for sale.

## Recommendations (Design Brief Starting Points)

1. **Core loop:** hub (dialogue, vendors, lore, challenge terminal) → linear mission of 20–40 minutes with ability-gated secrets → boss → rewards → hub. After the campaign, add a roguelite Rift mode.
2. **Combat spec:** light/heavy/jump/dodge/block/grab plus four powers per hero, energy resource, stagger meter on elites and bosses, damage types with auras from elite enemies, a team meter for pair combos (two heroes) and a squad ultimate (all four), and one-button hero swap.
3. **Progression spec:** automatic core stats; power points each level (respec-able); one or two signature relic slots per hero; a team-wide talent grid; shared XP for benched heroes (for example 75–100%); team-composition bonuses by origin realm; costumes with minor bonuses optional.
4. **Co-op spec:** 1–4 drop-in/drop-out, local plus online plus crossplay, a guest-pass model like Split Fiction's Friend Pass, and progress that carries over for joiners. AI teammates get simple, reliable command stances (aggressive, defensive, follow) and never path off ledges.
5. **Tech priorities:** camera and readability first, framerate stability, and VFX-density limits in four-player play. These are the most repeated technical complaints across twenty years.
6. **Scope control:** launch with 16–20 heroes spread across archetypes, not 36+. Depth per hero beats breadth, and expand after launch.

## Caveats

- MUA2 Metacritic figures (74 PS3 / 73 360) are confirmed by Metacritic's own listings. MUA1's 82 is the PS2 listing, and other platforms differ.
- MUA2's Fusion star cap (two versus three) and the MUA1 team-bonus caps come from community guides and weren't independently confirmed.
- Suicide Squad and Helldivers 2 player counts are SteamDB-derived (via VGC and Windows Central) and cover Steam only, not console players.
- "Extremis" appears only as an Iron Man costume in MUA2, not as a system. Any brief that lists it as MUA2 itemization is wrong.
- Sales and reception of 2025–2026 titles (Absolum, Cosmic Invasion) are still changing.
